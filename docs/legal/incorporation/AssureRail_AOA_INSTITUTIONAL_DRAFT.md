# Articles of Association of AssureRail Private Limited

**Institutional working draft for Indian corporate counsel and company-secretarial review — not for
filing or execution as-is**

**Prepared:** 6 September 2026

**Company form assumed:** private company limited by shares

**Status:** designed as an investable governance baseline; financing-specific rights remain open

## Drafting approach

These Articles supplement Table F with controls appropriate to an institutional financial-technology
infrastructure company. They protect the AssureRail IP and operating perimeter, make related-party
outsourcing reviewable, support equity/preference/convertible financing and establish information,
risk, cyber and continuity governance. They do not invent an investor veto, liquidation preference,
anti-dilution formula, founder vesting or exit right before those matters are negotiated.

The filing professional must convert the final version to INC-34/e-AOA format and check every clause
against the Companies Act, 2013, applicable rules and the final financing documents.

## THE COMPANIES ACT, 2013

### COMPANY LIMITED BY SHARES

### ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION

### OF

## ASSURERAIL PRIVATE LIMITED

## 1. Preliminary and interpretation

1.1 The regulations in Table F of Schedule I to the Companies Act, 2013 (the **Act**) apply except
to the extent excluded, modified or supplemented by these Articles.

1.2 In these Articles, unless the context otherwise requires:

- **Articles** means these articles of association as amended;
- **Board** means the board of directors of the Company;
- **Company** means AssureRail Private Limited or its finally approved name;
- **Founder** means Deepak Norman;
- **Institutional Financing** means an issuance to an institutional, venture-capital, private-equity,
  strategic or other professional investor approved in accordance with the Act;
- **Material Related-Party Arrangement** means an arrangement with a Founder, director, shareholder,
  group company or related party that meets a threshold approved by the Board or otherwise requires
  approval under the Act;
- **Rail IP** means the software, source code, schemas, models, specifications, workflows, brands,
  domains, documentation, data rights and other intellectual property owned or validly licensed by
  the Company for the AssureRail business;
- **Reserved Matter** means a matter designated under Article 12 or the terms of an issued class of
  securities; and
- **Securities** includes shares, preference shares, debentures, convertible instruments, options
  and other securities permitted by law.

1.3 References to writing include electronic records and signatures where permitted by law.
Headings do not affect interpretation. A reference to a statute includes lawful amendments and
replacement provisions. If these Articles conflict with the Act, the Act prevails.

## 2. Private-company status

2.1 The Company is a private company and accordingly:

- restricts the right to transfer its shares as provided in these Articles;
- limits its members to two hundred, subject to statutory exclusions; and
- prohibits any invitation to the public to subscribe for securities of the Company.

2.2 The Company shall not accept deposits or make an offer or allotment except in compliance with
the Act and applicable securities and financial-services law.

## 3. Share capital and classes

3.1 The authorised share capital is stated in the Memorandum. Subject to the Act, the Company may
issue equity shares, equity shares with differential rights, preference shares (including
compulsorily or optionally convertible, cumulative or non-cumulative, participating or
non-participating shares), debentures, warrants, options and other permitted Securities.

3.2 The rights of each class shall be set out in these Articles, the resolution approving the issue
or the definitive issue documents, and shall include as applicable voting, dividend, liquidation,
conversion, redemption, anti-dilution, information, nomination and consent rights.

3.3 No class right shall be varied except in accordance with the Act and the approval applicable to
that class. A consolidation, subdivision or reclassification shall not be used to defeat a class
right.

3.4 Shares may be issued for cash or lawful non-cash consideration at par or premium, subject to
valuation, tax, foreign-exchange, private-placement, preferential-allotment and other applicable
requirements.

## 4. Further issue, pre-emption and employee incentives

4.1 A further issue shall comply with section 62 of the Act. Except for an Exempt Issuance approved
under Article 4.3, new equity Securities shall first be offered to eligible existing holders in
proportion to their fully diluted holdings, on no more favourable economic terms than those offered
to a third party, with a response period fixed by law or the Board.

4.2 Securities not accepted in a pre-emptive offer may be issued within ninety days on terms no
more favourable to the recipient; otherwise they must be re-offered.

4.3 **Exempt Issuance** may include an employee-incentive plan within an approved pool, conversion of
outstanding Securities according to their terms, a bonus issue or share split, consideration in an
approved acquisition, or another issuance approved as a Reserved Matter.

4.4 The Company may establish employee stock-option, sweat-equity or other incentive plans under
the Act. The plan, pool size, eligibility, vesting, exercise, lapse and leaver rules require the
approvals prescribed by law and any applicable Reserved Matter consent.

## 5. Certificates, dematerialisation, calls, lien and forfeiture

5.1 Securities shall be certificated or held in dematerialised form as required or permitted by
law. The Company shall maintain statutory registers and reconcile them with the depository and
registrar where applicable.

5.2 Calls, lien, forfeiture, surrender, transmission and replacement of certificates are governed
by Table F and the Act, subject to the rights attached to each class.

5.3 No forfeiture, lien or administrative correction may be used to alter a negotiated class right
or evade a lawful transfer right.

## 6. Transfer restrictions and permitted transfers

6.1 No Security may be transferred except under the Act, these Articles and any binding class
terms incorporated into them. The Board may decline a non-compliant transfer, giving written
reasons and observing statutory deadlines.

6.2 A holder proposing a transfer shall give the Company a transfer notice stating the Securities,
proposed transferee, price and material terms. Unless it is a Permitted Transfer or otherwise
approved as a Reserved Matter, the Securities shall first be offered pro rata to the other eligible
holders on the same terms for twenty-one days.

6.3 Unaccepted Securities may be transferred to the disclosed transferee within ninety days at a
price not lower and on terms no more favourable than offered to existing holders. A material change
requires a new offer.

6.4 A **Permitted Transfer** is a transfer approved by all holders entitled to the pre-emption right,
or a transfer by an individual to a controlled family trust or wholly owned holding vehicle for
estate planning, or by an institutional holder to an affiliate or successor fund, provided that:

- the transferee signs a deed of adherence to the applicable governance arrangements;
- beneficial ownership and control are transparently disclosed;
- the transfer does not breach law, sanctions, competition, licence, customer or security
  requirements; and
- the Securities transfer back if the transferee ceases to qualify.

6.5 The final financing documents may add right-of-first-refusal, tag-along, drag-along, lock-in,
founder-transfer and exit provisions. They shall take corporate effect only after the required
provisions are incorporated into these Articles and approved under the Act.

## 7. Transmission

Transmission by operation of law is governed by the Act and Table F. The Board may require
reasonable evidence and shall protect the rights attached to the relevant class.

## 8. General meetings, voting and written/electronic participation

8.1 General meetings, notice, quorum, proxies, polls, electronic voting and participation through
video conferencing or other audio-visual means shall comply with the Act and applicable standards.

8.2 Voting is proportionate to paid-up voting equity unless a class lawfully carries different
rights. Interested members shall abstain where required by law.

8.3 Minutes and voting records shall identify the resolution, materials supplied, conflicts,
abstentions and result and be retained as required by law.

## 9. Board composition and nomination

9.1 The Company shall have not fewer than two and not more than fifteen directors unless the Act
permits and the Company approves another maximum.

9.2 The first directors are **[●]**. Deepak Norman is intended to serve as a Founder Director,
subject to appointment, retirement, removal and other requirements of the Act and any future
financing terms.

9.3 A holder or class may nominate a director or observer only where that right has been lawfully
created in the terms of issue and these Articles. An observer has no vote and may be excluded from
privileged, conflicted, competitively sensitive or security-sensitive discussions.

9.4 The Board shall collectively maintain competence appropriate to a financial-infrastructure
technology company, including product/domain, technology, information security, finance, legal or
regulatory, risk and institutional-customer experience as the Company matures.

## 10. Board proceedings and decision integrity

10.1 The Board may meet in person or through permitted electronic means. Notice, agenda and
materials shall ordinarily be circulated at least seven days before a meeting, with shorter notice
only as allowed by the Act and accompanied by a recorded reason.

10.2 Quorum is two directors or one-third of total strength, whichever is higher, including any
class-nominated director whose presence is required by valid class terms. A quorum requirement
shall not create indefinite paralysis: an adjourned-meeting mechanism must be specified in the
relevant class terms.

10.3 Questions are decided by majority unless the Act, these Articles or a class right requires a
higher threshold. The chair has no casting vote on a Reserved Matter, conflict transaction,
Founder transaction, security incident attribution or class-right matter.

10.4 Circular resolutions must comply with the Act and shall not be used to avoid required
discussion of a material conflict, insolvency risk, security event or Reserved Matter.

10.5 Board minutes shall record materials considered, dissent, conflicts, recusals and material
control or risk conclusions. Confidential Board access shall be role-based and auditable.

## 11. Delegation, committees and executive authority

11.1 The Board may delegate authority to committees, officers or agents under a written authority
matrix stating scope, limits, duration, reporting and revocation.

11.2 The Board may establish audit and finance, risk and compliance, information-security and
technology, nomination and remuneration, conduct and customer-outcomes, or other committees. A
committee does not displace statutory director responsibility.

11.3 Material payments, changes to production capability, cryptographic-key control, privileged
access, customer-data export, evidence override and reconciliation closure shall use segregation
of duties and maker-checker controls proportionate to risk.

11.4 Emergency authority may be used to contain a cyber, safety, regulatory or continuity event,
but must be limited, logged, independently reviewed and reported promptly to the Board.

## 12. Reserved matters

12.1 The following require Board approval and, once an Institutional Financing grants a valid class
consent, that class consent at the threshold stated in its terms:

- amendment of the Memorandum or Articles or variation of class rights;
- issue, buy-back, redemption, conversion or reorganisation of Securities other than an approved
  issuance;
- adoption or material increase of an employee-equity pool;
- merger, demerger, sale of substantially all assets, change of control, liquidation or winding up;
- acquisition, disposal, exclusive licence, encumbrance or abandonment of material Rail IP;
- entry into or material amendment of a Material Related-Party Arrangement;
- borrowing, guarantee, capital expenditure, acquisition or disposal above Board-approved limits;
- material change to the nature of the business or entry into a regulated function outside the
  approved regulatory-perimeter plan;
- approval or material change of the annual business plan or budget;
- appointment or removal of the chief executive, chief financial, chief technology, chief
  information-security or chief compliance/risk officer after those offices are established;
- appointment/removal of statutory auditor or material change in accounting policy;
- commencement or settlement of material litigation or admission of material liability;
- dividend or distribution outside an approved policy; and
- insolvency filing, cessation of a material business line or other matter lawfully designated in
  the terms of a class.

12.2 Before Institutional Financing, Reserved Matters are decided under the Act and Board/member
thresholds otherwise applicable. This Article creates capacity for negotiated investor protection;
it does not grant an unidentified future investor a present right.

## 13. Regulatory perimeter and capability activation

13.1 The Company shall maintain a Board-approved register of material product functions identifying
the legal route, operating mode, function performer, required licence or partner, evidence gate and
activation authority.

13.2 Code presence, a feature flag, sandbox, replay or demonstration does not establish legal
permission or production readiness. A controlled-live or production function may be activated only
after the approvals, external evidence, security, operational and customer gates prescribed by the
Board-approved policy are satisfied.

13.3 The Company shall not hold client money, securities, private keys controlling customer assets,
or an authoritative ownership register, or perform trustee, exchange, depository, clearing,
settlement, custody, payment, rating, advice or other regulated functions unless lawfully approved
and expressly authorised by the Board.

13.4 Where another institution or authorised provider performs a regulated or legally operative
act, the Company's systems shall preserve that performer's acknowledgement and shall not silently
substitute the Company's internal record for the authoritative record.

## 14. Intellectual property and critical assets

14.1 The Company shall own or hold sufficient written rights to all material Rail IP used in its
business. Contributor, contractor and employee agreements shall include present assignments,
confidentiality, further-assurance and permitted moral-right treatment to the extent lawful.

14.2 The Founder-to-Company assignment, its baseline schedules, exclusions, open-source register
and delivery certificate shall be approved and retained as permanent corporate records.

14.3 Material Rail IP, source repositories, domains, cloud subscriptions, signing identities,
artefact registries, customer contracts and operational evidence shall be controlled by the Company
or under an express, auditable and exit-capable contract. No service provider or related party shall
be indispensable merely because it holds an undocumented credential or account.

14.4 No material Rail IP may be transferred, exclusively licensed, pledged, abandoned or made
subject to a copyleft or source-disclosure obligation outside approved policy without Reserved
Matter approval.

## 15. Related parties, outsourcing and conflicts

15.1 Every director shall disclose interests and comply with recusal and approval obligations under
the Act. The Company shall maintain a related-party register and evidence arm's-length terms where
required.

15.2 Any appointment of AssureLocker or another Founder-related entity to develop, operate, support
or supply services to AssureRail must be a separate written agreement approved through the
applicable conflict and related-party process. AssureLocker has no founding ownership, implied
licence, veto or indispensable administrative right in AssureRail by reason of common involvement.

15.3 A material outsourcing agreement shall state services, service levels, fees, data-processing
role, information-security obligations, audit/access rights, subcontracting, business continuity,
incident handling, IP ownership, personnel/key separation, termination assistance, portability and
exit. Outsourcing does not transfer the Board's accountability.

15.4 A director with a material interest shall not vote or count for quorum to the extent required
by law or applicable approval terms.

## 16. Information security, privacy, resilience and records

16.1 The Board shall approve proportionate frameworks for cybersecurity, privacy and data
protection, identity and access, secure development, vulnerability management, cryptographic keys,
third-party risk, logging, incident response, business continuity, disaster recovery, record
retention, legal hold and customer exit.

16.2 Material incidents, breaches, control failures, reconciliation breaks and regulatory or
customer notifications shall be escalated under a documented severity matrix. Closure of a
material finding shall be evidenced and independently reviewed.

16.3 The Company shall commission independent testing and assurance appropriate to its maturity and
contracts. No Article or internal certification shall be represented as a statutory licence or
regulatory approval.

16.4 Customer and transaction data shall be processed only on a lawful and documented basis. The
Company shall distinguish its own corporate records from customer-owned or third-party evidence.

## 17. Business plan, accounts, audit and information rights

17.1 The Board shall approve an annual business plan, operating budget, capital plan and material
risk appetite and review performance against them.

17.2 The Company shall maintain books, statutory registers, tax records and financial statements and
appoint auditors in accordance with law.

17.3 Each member receives statutory information. A class or institutional holder may receive
additional periodic financial, operating, risk and inspection rights only as lawfully created in
its class terms, subject to confidentiality, legal privilege, data protection, security and
competitive-sensitivity safeguards.

17.4 Information rights do not confer access to customer data, cryptographic secrets, privileged
material or another participant's confidential transaction evidence except where lawfully
authorised.

## 18. Borrowing, guarantees and treasury

Subject to the Act and Reserved Matters, the Board may borrow, issue debt Securities, create charges
and guarantees and manage cash and investments for the Company's business. The Company shall not
carry on banking, deposit-taking or financing as its principal business without required approval.

## 19. Dividends and reserves

Dividends and reserves are governed by the Act, Table F, class rights, solvency and financing
covenants. No distribution may prejudice statutory liabilities, customer obligations, security
remediation or approved continuity requirements.

## 20. Notices and electronic records

Notices may be delivered personally, by post, courier or permitted electronic means. The Company
shall maintain evidence of delivery and current contact information. A failure caused by an
unnotified address change does not invalidate otherwise lawful notice.

## 21. Confidentiality, privilege and public statements

21.1 Directors, observers, officers and members receiving non-public information shall keep it
confidential, use it only for the proper purpose for which it was supplied and apply appropriate
security, subject to lawful disclosure duties.

21.2 Public claims concerning licences, transaction completion, assurance grade, customer adoption,
security certification, deployment or performance require evidence and approval under Company
policy.

21.3 Legal privilege and regulatory confidentiality shall be preserved; broad information rights
do not waive either.

## 22. Indemnity and insurance

To the extent permitted by law, the Company may indemnify directors and officers and maintain
directors' and officers' or other appropriate insurance. No indemnity covers fraud, wilful
misconduct, unlawful personal benefit or another matter prohibited by law.

## 23. Common seal and execution

The Company may adopt a common seal. Custody and use of any seal and execution of documents shall
comply with the Act and the Board-approved authority matrix.

## 24. Winding up

On winding up, assets remaining after satisfaction of liabilities shall be applied according to the
Act and rights attached to each class. Customer property, confidential information and regulated
records shall be returned, transferred, preserved or destroyed in accordance with law and contract
and shall not be treated as freely distributable Company property.

## 25. Relationship with shareholders' agreements

The Company and members may enter into a shareholders' agreement. A provision intended to bind the
Company corporately or regulate transfer, voting, Board or class rights shall be incorporated into
these Articles to the extent counsel considers necessary. If a conflict remains, the Act prevails,
then the Memorandum and these Articles; the parties shall exercise lawful rights to amend the
documents so far as possible to give effect to their agreed commercial intent.

## 26. Amendment and severability

These Articles may be amended only under the Act and applicable class rights. An invalid or
unenforceable provision shall be read down where lawful without invalidating the remainder.
