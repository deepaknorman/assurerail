# AssureRail pilot pricing and commercial schedule

**Status:** confidential working draft; prospect use requires founder/commercial approval

**Version:** 1.0 · 7 September 2026

**Currency:** INR unless the signed order form states otherwise

**Tax:** applicable GST and statutory levies are additional

## 1. Pricing doctrine

AssureRail is not a universally fixed-fee service. Pricing should follow the function, route,
operating mode, work required and permitted fee basis. A replay can be fixed-scope; platform and
transaction services may combine implementation, recurring and variable fees.

Current founder-approved planning rates for PTC are:

- **conventional PTC:** 0.30% (30 bps) of the contractually defined fee base; and
- **tokenised PTC:** 0.50% (50 bps) of the contractually defined fee base, only if and when that
  route is lawfully available and activated.

These rates do not define the fee base, payer, trigger, minimum, cap, refund, reversal treatment or
tax. Every order form must define those terms. They are not public list prices and must not be
presented as current live-product availability.

## 2. Commercial building blocks

| Component | Pricing basis | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery workshop | fixed | May be waived or credited only with written approval |
| Completed-deal replay | fixed scoped fee | Prospect-specific after evidence-family and complexity review |
| Shadow evaluation | fixed programme fee plus approved integration effort | No transaction-execution fee for observe-only activity unless expressly agreed |
| Implementation/integration | fixed milestone or time-and-materials cap | Scope by systems, formats, environments, testing and support |
| Conventional DA platform fee | order-form specific | Rate remains a commercial decision; do not infer the PTC rate |
| Conventional PTC platform fee | 30 bps of defined fee base | Subject to permission, trigger, minimum/cap and contract |
| Tokenised PTC platform fee | 50 bps of defined fee base | Future, separately gated; never quote as presently available |
| Lifecycle/monitoring | annual/programme, per case or volume tier | Define coverage, frequency, evidence providers and service level |
| Assurance or professional opinion | separate appointment and fee | Never bundle as an outcome-dependent Rail fee; provider may be independent of AssureRail |
| Third-party charges | pass-through or direct customer contract | Name provider, markup (if any), tax and failure/refund treatment |

## 3. Required order-form variables

No proposal is send-ready until it states:

1. contracting entity and customer legal name;
2. service mode and route;
3. included functions and explicit exclusions;
4. fee base and excluded amounts;
5. rate/bps, fixed amounts, minimum and cap;
6. earned/billing/payment trigger;
7. cancellation, failure, reversal and refund/credit treatment;
8. implementation assumptions and change-control rates;
9. third-party charges and taxes;
10. invoice timing, payment period and disputed-invoice process;
11. service period, renewal and termination assistance; and
12. authorised commercial approver and approval date.

## 4. Illustrative calculation

For a defined conventional PTC fee base of INR 300 crore:

`INR 300 crore × 30 bps = INR 0.90 crore`, before any contractual minimum, cap, tax or adjustment.

This arithmetic is illustrative, not a quote or revenue forecast. The companion workbook retains
the fee base in rupees and calculates exact paise to avoid crore/million interpretation errors.

## 5. Approval and disclosure controls

- public web pages state “commercial terms by scope”; they do not publish the PTC planning rates;
- a proposal PDF must carry version, validity period, confidentiality label and approving person;
- assurance-provider fees and Rail fees are separately stated;
- discounts record reason, approver, expiry and whether they affect later renewals;
- no fee is described as earned by transaction success unless counsel and the contract approve that
  trigger for the precise function; and
- no model output or synthetic demonstration is used to substantiate savings or return claims.
