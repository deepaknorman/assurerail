MethodFive moves

Do not rebut immediately. Locate the control they are protecting.

1

Acknowledge

Confirm the concern is legitimate.

2

Diagnose

Ask which system, duty or failure they mean.

3

Answer

State the exact product boundary.

4

Prove

Offer an artefact or pilot test.

5

Advance

Ask for the smallest next commitment.

The goal is not to “win” the objection. It is to turn an abstract risk into a testable pilot proposition.
Never answer a regulatory objection with architecture alone, or an operating objection with a legal-entity diagram alone.
Field guide
QualificationBefore the pitch

A qualified opportunity has a transaction, an accountable owner and a measurable break.

Route and flow

  • DA, PTC or both?
  • Primary, subsequent transfer or secondary trade?
  • Repeat asset class and expected flow?

Authority and systems

  • Who is trustee/arranger/investor?
  • Which system creates legal completion?
  • Who owns each current hand-off?

Proof and sponsor

  • One closed case available?
  • Named risk/ops/tech owners?
  • What must be measurably better?
Strong: repeat flow + visible reconciliation painWeak: “show us tokenisation” with no route owner
Do not start solution design from a generic innovation or blockchain brief. Start from a transaction and its accountable parties.
Field guide
Objection 01Existing platform

“We already use Yubi, an arranger platform and our own systems.”

Risk underneath: replacement cost, disruption and an unproven new dependency.

What is true

Those platforms may already perform essential origination, arrangement or workflow functions.

Answer

“Keep them. We begin with one replay and shadow case across them. Your incumbent remains authoritative.”

Do not say

“We replace the arranger,” “one platform for everything,” or “migration is easy.”

Next question

“Where do you currently reconcile tape, documents, payment, trustee and holding records?”

Proof offered: completed-deal replay + system-of-record map + comparison workbook. No production integration.
The coexistence wedge is stronger when the incumbent participates in the first consortium rather than being treated as an enemy.

Objection 02Workflow commodity

“This sounds like another deal room or workflow tool.”

Risk underneath: no differentiated economic or control value.

What is true

A portion of the UX will resemble workflow because institutions need tasks, evidence and approvals.

Answer

The product is the governed case: authority, evidence provenance, deterministic gates, external acknowledgements and legal-record reconciliation.

Do not say

“Blockchain makes it unique,” or “single source of truth” when an external register is operative.

Next question

“Can your present room deterministically replay who decided, on which evidence and what made completion operative?”

Proof offered: rerun the same case twice; export it; reconcile the final state to the external register.
Lead with control outcomes and portability, not UI novelty.

Objection 03Regulation and licence

“Are you licensed to operate this venue?”

Risk underneath: unlawful venue activity and institutional liability for participating.

What is true

AssureRail is not presented as holding a generic venue licence. Live permission is function- and mode-specific.

Answer

“Replay/shadow performs no live matching or legal-record write. Live functions will be AssureRail-owned only when authorised, otherwise partner-executed.”

Do not say

“We are only software, so regulation does not apply,” or “invite-only makes it unregulated.”

Next question

“Which function concerns you—term display, RFQ, matching, issuance, secondary execution, settlement or recordkeeping?”

Proof offered: mode/function licensed-performer matrix reviewed by transaction counsel and the relevant partner.
Primary SDI EBP, secondary RFQ/reporting/clearing, DA transfer and IT-outsourcing questions are different analyses—not one licence.

Objection 04Company structure

“A separate AssureRail company does not solve the conflict.”

Risk underneath: form without operating substance.

What is true

Correct. Incorporation alone does not create independence or compliance.

Answer

It creates real contracting, liability, capital, governance, inspection and funding separation—then operational controls make it substantive.

Do not say

“Ring-fenced, so AssureLocker has no exposure,” or “separate company eliminates all conflicts.”

Next question

“Which separation matters most to you: assurance, data, people, fees, decisions, capital or incident accountability?”

Proof offered: separate RACI, access/keys, contracts, fee records, board accountabilities, reconciliations, incident routes and provider-exit test.
Planning presumes a separate domestic AssureRail OpCo before regulated live venue activity; exact IP, funding and ownership remain for counsel.

Objection 05Assurance conflict

“Are you assuring transactions on your own venue?”

Risk underneath: commercial pressure could suppress or alter adverse findings.

What is true

The trustee or accountable party organises the assurance. AssurePlane, the AssureCLA assurance product, is optional.

Answer

AssureRail validates the appointment, scope, signature, freshness and evidence object. It does not convert it into its own grade.

Do not say

“AssurePlane is independent because it is in another division,” or imply compulsory routing.

Next question

“Who appoints and can replace the reviewer today, and what exact output does the trustee require?”

Proof offered: provider-neutral schema, signed appointment, related-party disclosure, access separation and substitution/export exercise.
Independent assurance fees remain separately commissioned and outcome-independent.

Objection 05AExisting identity and access stack

“We already have SSO, KYC and user administration.”

Risk underneath: duplicate onboarding, inconsistent authority and unnecessary dependence on another group product.

What is true

Your IdP and approved KYC/KYB providers should remain usable. A second identity database is not the customer value.

Answer

AssureRail adds transaction authority: legal entity, membership, mandate, maker-checker, route role, limits, connector certification and suspension.

Do not say

“DigiKYC onboarding makes the institution eligible,” or require an AssureLocker product merely to participate.

Next question

“Which system proves identity, which record proves signing authority, and who suspends a person or institution today?”

Proof offered: federated/provider-neutral evidence intake, two-person mandate rehearsal, route-scoped activation, immediate suspension and tested exit/export.
Rail-local institution, membership, mandate, appointment and entitlement controls are implemented behind disabled controls. Real identity-provider evidence, assignments, enforcement rehearsal and customer acceptance remain open.

Objection 06Trustee and depository

“Whose record wins if AssureRail and the depository disagree?”

Risk underneath: double ownership truth and an invalid completion.

What is true

The trustee is final for the PTC workflow; the route-defined depository/RTA/register retains legal force.

Answer

AssureRail freezes. The trustee accepts/rejects and directs correction through the operative recordkeeper. Release follows acknowledgement.

Do not say

“The trustee’s email overrides the depository,” or “AssureRail is the single legal source of truth.”

Next question

“For this placement and holding form, which record legally establishes the holder and when?”

Proof offered: deliberately inject a mismatch and demonstrate freeze, correction authority, acknowledgement and before/after reconciliation.
No internal status, token or informal message is permitted to create a competing title record.

Objection 07Tokenisation

“We do not want blockchain or tokens.”

Risk underneath: regulatory novelty, custody/key risk and forced technology change.

What is true

Conventional DA and PTC are first-class modes and lead in shadow. Tokenisation is not required.

Answer

“Use AssureRail conventionally. Add a token representation only where the route is authorised and it creates measurable value.”

Do not say

“Tokens are safer,” “token transfer equals title,” or “CBDC makes settlement automatic.”

Next question

“Which current reconciliation, secondary-transfer or settlement problem would tokenisation need to improve?”

Proof offered: conventional file/API replay and shadow. Token adapter excluded from scope unless separately requested and gated.
“Conventional” may still be end-to-end digital, API-enabled, signed and tamper-evident.

Objection 08Scope breadth

“DA and PTC are too different for one product.”

Risk underneath: a generic workflow will flatten legally important differences.

What is true

They are different routes. PTC is not “DA plus a certificate.”

Answer

One common kernel covers evidence, authority, state, audit and reconciliation; separate route adapters own parties, documents, gates and lifecycle.

Do not say

“Same process with different templates,” or imply AssurePool structures PTC.

Next question

“Which controls are genuinely common, and which PTC trustee/issue/lifecycle duties must never be abstracted?”

Proof offered: replay one of each; customer validates the implemented common-versus-route-specific control map before shadow acceptance.
AssurePool is DA-only and optional. PTC enters AssureRail directly.

Objection 09Credit decision

“Are you deciding which loans or pools are acceptable?”

Risk underneath: unlawful outsourcing of lender judgement and hidden investment advice.

What is true

The transferee owns DA diligence and credit decision; the investor owns its investment decision.

Answer

AssureRail organises facts, terms, evidence, exceptions and approvals. It records—not replaces—the participant decision.

Do not say

“Approved by AssureRail,” “investment grade,” or “eligible” without the rule, authority and decision owner.

Next question

“Which facts can be computed centrally, and which policy/judgement must remain inside your team?”

Proof offered: decision-rights matrix and separate records for platform control, participant decision and external opinion.
RBI’s transfer framework states that the transferee’s loan due diligence cannot be outsourced.

Objection 10Data and security

“We cannot put borrower or transaction data on another platform.”

Risk underneath: confidentiality, residency, cyber concentration and regulator access.

What is true

Real-data use needs classification, minimisation, India-residency design, contracts, VAPT and access/retention controls. Those external gates are open.

Answer

Start redacted/file-based. The selected application target is Azure India—Hyderabad primary and Pune recovery—subject to service-by-service design, VAPT and exercised failover. Keep privileged-source access inside participant boundaries where required. Never put borrower PII on an immutable ledger.

Do not say

“Hashes mean there is no privacy risk,” “blockchain is immutable so it is secure,” or claim certifications not obtained.

Next question

“Which fields cannot leave, which can be tokenised/redacted, and which acknowledgement can be referenced rather than copied?”

Proof offered: field-level data map, threat model, role/access demonstration, audit export, restore and provider-exit test.
Security is a production gate, not a marketing adjective. Shadow scope should minimise data before expanding integrations.

Objection 11Liquidity and network

“A venue without buyers and sellers has no value.”

Risk underneath: cold-start, fragmented liquidity and dependence on speculative future adoption.

What is true

Matching value requires participant density. AssureRail does not yet claim an active liquidity network.

Pre-liquidity value comes from controlled transaction state, evidence, reconciliation and lifecycle. First flow comes from a committed consortium and incumbent channels.

Do not say

“Build it and liquidity will come,” or cite total securitisation flow as captive AssureRail volume.

Next question

“Which repeat programme already has originator, investor and trustee demand but suffers execution friction?”

Proof offered: committed pipeline and participant roles before matching build; partner-executed RFQ first; repeat-programme conversion criterion.
Do not launch an anonymous order book first. Institutional invite-only RFQ is the working hypothesis, subject to permission.

Objection 12Integration and burden

“Our technology team has no capacity for another integration.”

Risk underneath: an innovation pilot becoming an uncontrolled enterprise programme.

What is true

A production rail will eventually require serious integrations and operating ownership.

Answer

Replay and first shadow are file-in/workbook-out. API is a later carrier over the same schema, not a prerequisite.

Do not say

“Zero integration forever,” “plug and play,” or conceal security/procurement work required for live use.

Next question

“Can operations export one closed case and name the person who knows each file?”

Proof offered: signed manifest, reference templates, rejected-record report, deterministic outputs and incumbent mapping—before API discussion.
Make the initial ask small enough for the customer to say yes without borrowing an engineering roadmap.

Objection 13Fees and neutrality

“A transaction-linked fee will bias the platform toward closure.”

Risk underneath: suppressed exceptions, conflicted admission or distorted matching.

What is true

Fee design can create conduct conflicts. A separate company does not make a percentage fee safe.

Answer

Replay/shadow are fixed-scope. Venue economics are route/function-specific, disclosed and counsel-approved. Current PTC illustrations are 0.30% conventional and 0.50% tokenised; neither is a hard-coded default. Assurance is separately appointed and outcome-independent.

Do not say

“Percentage fees are allowed because exchanges charge them,” or blur assurance and venue invoices.

Next question

“Which action is being paid for, by whom, and does payment depend on acceptance, execution, value or completion?”

Proof offered: fee map by function, payer, trigger, cap, cancellation/refund, disclosure and related-party treatment.
Fixed versus percentage is a conduct/commercial question, not the reason to incorporate AssureRail.

Objection 14Track record

“You have no production venue track record.”

Risk underneath: institutional, operational and continuity risk—not merely software maturity.

What is true

Correct. The generic DA/PTC product journeys are built and deployed behind disabled controls, but there is no customer-accepted or controlled-live production venue.

Answer

“Do not trust a production claim. Judge a replay, shadow comparison, failure test and complete export against your own cases.”

Do not say

“Live,” “production-ready,” “atomic,” or cite built/deployed code or a working endpoint as market-infrastructure proof.

Next question

“What evidence would let your risk, operations and technology owners approve the next—not final—stage?”

Proof offered: four-part programme with signed gates, independent participant review and the ability to stop after every part.
Honest status is a sales asset in regulated infrastructure. Never trade it for false immediacy.

DisqualifiersKnow when to stop

Not every prospect should become an AssureRail pilot.

Proceed when

  • There is repeat DA/PTC flow and a named executive sponsor.
  • Risk, operations and technology owners will participate.
  • A closed case and current process artefacts are available.
  • The customer accepts replay/shadow before live claims.
  • The trustee/recordkeeper will define authority and reconciliation.

Stop or hold when

  • They want a token demo but no legal transaction owner.
  • They require AssureRail to make or outsource the credit decision.
  • They want undisclosed matching or use of another party’s data.
  • No one will own register, settlement or lifecycle breaks.
  • They demand production claims before security/legal gates.
A disciplined “not yet” protects the regulator relationship, product credibility and the first reference customer.
Do not expand the pilot to rescue a weak sponsor. Narrow it until accountability and proof are clear.
